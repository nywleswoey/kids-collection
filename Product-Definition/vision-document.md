## Executive Summary

**Star Catchers** (repo `kids-collection`) is a private, kid-safe collectible-card web app, live on
Vercel and used daily by one family: a parent and three children aged 4, 7 and 9. It is a significant
addition to an existing product, not a new build — 22 increments shipped between 2026-07-11 and
2026-08-01. Its core capability is turning a parent-granted token into a rarity-weighted card pull from
a pre-generated, parent-reviewed pool, and giving each child a persistent personal binder to grow,
complete and trade from. The problem it solves is a parenting one rather than a commercial one: making
screen time earnable and educational, with a hard guarantee that nothing unreviewed ever reaches a
child. Success is measured by a single metric — runtime AI cost held at **$0/month** as the card pool
grows.

## Business Context

### Problem Statement

Screen time was a bargaining chip with no structure behind it: the parent had nothing to trade against
that the kids actually wanted, and the things they *did* want (games, videos) taught them nothing and
cost money or attention to police. The parent also can't hand three young children an open AI tool —
anything generated has to be reviewed before a 4-year-old sees it, and no commercial kids' collectible
app gives that guarantee while also being free to run.

Concretely, what hurt: (a) no reward currency the parent controlled, (b) no way to make that reward
educational, (c) unreviewed AI output is unacceptable for this age group, (d) per-pull AI image and text
generation would cost real money at kid-scale pull rates.

### Business Drivers

No external driver and no hard date. This is a personal project built for the parent's own three

weeks. The "why now" is developmental rather than commercial: the children are at the ages where a
collecting loop lands, with the 4-year-old at the picture-driven stage and the 9-year-old at the

### Business Constraints

- **Budget cap — effectively $0 recurring.** No runtime AI spend: the pool is pre-generated, and card
  text is authored offline via claude.ai so no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is needed at runtime. Image generation
  uses a free, no-API-key service (Pollinations.ai). Hosting and storage stay within free tiers
  (Vercel + Neon Postgres + Vercel Blob).
- **Kid-safety policy — inviolable, not a preference.** Every image and every line of text is
  parent-reviewed before it can reach a child. No live unreviewed AI output. No external links or open
  input surfaces on any child-facing screen.
- **Engineering policy carried from v1 as hard constraints.** Security Baseline and Property-Based
  Testing are **blocking**; Resiliency is **directional** (design-time guidance).
- **Not applicable — regulatory/audit**: no external regulation applies. Private family app, no
  third-party data.

## Full Scope Vision

### Product Vision Statement

> Every card a child in this family opens is a small, safe moment of delight that also teaches them
> something true — and the collection they build is theirs, permanent, and worth showing off.

> ⚠️ **Provenance**: AI-synthesised and accepted verbatim; v1 has no vision statement. See **OQ-B-3**.

### Feature Areas

Grounded in the repository as of 2026-08-03 — 14 feature modules under `src/features/`, 16 routes.

- **Auth & profiles** — Google OAuth (parent only, email allowlist) → "who's playing?" child picker;
  profile choice is a cookie convenience, not a security boundary.
- **Pull engine** — spend a token, rarity-weighted draw from the shared pool; category chips
  (🎲 Random + the 8 newest themes); may branch into an easter-egg offer.
- **Ticket economy** — normal `pullTokens`, special egg tickets (✨ epic / 🍀 lucky), and per-rarity pick
  tickets. The single currency the whole reward loop runs on.
- **Binder / Galaxy** — per-child collection grouped by theme, duplicate counts `xN`, locked silhouettes
  for unowned cards, rarity and category filters, per-theme completion progress.
- **Card rendering & effects** — holographic foil, 3D tilt/parallax, rarity-scaled intensity, pack-open
  reveal, roulette.
- **Sound** — SFX and BGM, rarity fanfares, dedicated easter-egg cue.
- **Easter eggs** — ~1% pick-1-of-N epic+ offers, HMAC-signed and expiring so a claim can't be swapped.
- **Quizzes / Learn** — educational questions with per-question feedback (correct/incorrect, the right
  answer, and a "why"); award granted server-side via signed offer.
- **Trading** — atomic two-sided duplicate swap between two children; friend-first board that badges only
  what the other party lacks.
- **Sacrifice** — burn 4+ copies (keep 1) for a rarity-pick ticket, same tier or one up, 50/50.
- **Set-completion rewards** — bonus card once per (child, theme, rarity) set completed, cascading.
- **Admin** — passcode-gated (20s sliding TTL): manage profiles, grant every ticket type, view any
  child's binder, preview and curate the pool.
- **Seeding pipeline** — offline: author card text via a claude.ai prompt → `seed/cards.json` → generate
  images programmatically → upload to Blob → load into Postgres. Parent-reviewable before publish.

**Current pool**: 12 themes × 30 cards = **360 cards** (Increment 24 added Flying Machines and Ocean
Machines, the first man-made subject matter), each theme a uniform 15 common / 8 rare /
5 epic / 2 legendary.

### Future Extensions (not committed)

- **Token-grant history / audit for kids** — an admin nicety, deferred.
- **Higher-quality image generation** — Cloudflare Workers AI / Flux instead of Pollinations.ai, parked
  because it requires an account.

Two further items were considered during definition and are now **settled rather than pending**:
read-aloud / text-to-speech is **declined** ("pictures alone is sufficient"), and re-tuning the sacrifice
threshold is **withdrawn** (the current value works as designed).

### Non-Functional Priorities for MVP

Three properties matter most, and two commonly-assumed ones explicitly do not.

- **Security and data protection**, in the specific shape of *kid-safety*: nothing unreviewed ever
  reaches a child, and no child-side privilege escalation — a child must not be able to grant themselves
  tickets. Security Baseline is a blocking constraint.
- **Cost efficiency** — the $0/month runtime target that is also the product's single success metric.
- **Usability for a 4-year-old pre-reader** — a harder constraint here than latency or scale: large touch
  targets, image-forward layouts, minimal text, and honouring `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Explicit non-goals**: **scalability** is irrelevant at three users in one family, and **availability**
has no target worth stating — nobody is paged if the app is down for an hour. **Latency** matters only as
a feel requirement ("effects stay smooth, ~60fps, degrading gracefully on low-end devices"), not as a
service-level objective. These are recorded as deliberate non-goals so that no future work designs for
load or uptime that will never be needed.

## Current State

Star Catchers is a private, kid-safe collectible-card web app live on Vercel, used daily by one parent
and three children aged 4, 7 and 9. The parent signs in with Google (email allowlist) and grants tickets;
each child picks their profile and spends tickets to pull rarity-weighted cards from a shared,
pre-generated pool of 360 cards across 12 themes (uniformly 15 common / 8 rare / 5 epic / 2 legendary per
theme), each card carrying an image and an age-appropriate educational fact. Cards land in a per-child
binder ("Galaxy") grouped by theme with duplicate counts and locked silhouettes, and can be viewed with
holographic, 3D-tilt and reveal effects. Around that core sit quizzes, kid-to-kid trading, duplicate
sacrifice, easter-egg offers, set-completion rewards and a passcode-gated admin area. Technically it is a
Next.js App Router + TypeScript + Tailwind application on Vercel, with Neon Postgres (Drizzle, migrations
0000–0006 applied to production) and Vercel Blob for images; 22 increments shipped between 2026-07-11 and
2026-08-01, with 206 tests green.

## What Must NOT Change

⚠️ **Hard boundary for AI-DLC.** Each item below is a constraint on all future work.

- **The children's existing collection data — above all else.** `collections` rows are the accumulated
  result of every pull the kids have ever made. They cannot be reset, re-seeded, or lost to a migration.
  Any future migration touching `collections`, `children`, or `cards` requires an explicit
  data-preservation plan.
- **Atomicity contracts on the ticket/card economy.** `count_at_least_one` — `CHECK(count >= 1)` on
  `collections` (BR9); duplicates decrement, never to zero-rows. Also `pull_tokens_non_negative`,
  `easter_egg_tickets_non_negative`, and the epic/lucky ticket non-negative checks. `spendOne` returns
  null on guard failure (no double-spend); `clampedGrant` floors at 0; `swapCards` is all-or-nothing —
  a trade must never half-apply.
- **Server-authoritative awards via HMAC-signed offers.** Easter-egg picks and quiz awards are pinned in
  a signed, expiring offer so a claim cannot be swapped for an un-offered card. Client-side answer keys
  exist for feedback only. Award decisions must never move to the client.
- **`themes.sort_order` is a contract, not a convenience.** Backfilled in migration 0006 to the exact
  order the children already saw. Reordering silently reshuffles their world.
- **The auth and safety boundary.** Parent Google OAuth + email allowlist is the only real boundary.
  Child profile selection is an httpOnly cookie convenience, **not** a security boundary — it must never
  be treated as one. Admin actions stay behind the passcode gate. No secret may reach the client bundle.
- **Kid-safety: no unreviewed content path to a child, ever.** Every image and fact is parent-reviewed
  before going live. No runtime generation into a child-facing surface.
- **The review→publish byte identity.** Since Increment 24, review filenames are content-addressed by
  prompt hash and `pnpm seed --sync` publishes the reviewed *bytes*. `--sync` generating a fresh image
  for a card that has a matching review file is a **regression, not an optimisation** — it re-opens the
  gap where an edited prompt could republish an image reviewed against a prompt that no longer exists.
  `--sync` also refuses to insert a card with no reviewed image without `--allow-unreviewed`.
- **`SACRIFICE_COST` / `SACRIFICE_MIN` stay a single source of truth.** `SACRIFICE_COST = 3`,
  `SACRIFICE_MIN = 4` in `src/features/pull/sacrifice.ts`, consumed by both the card detail page and the
  galaxy filter, with a property-based test asserting the equivalence. The values are correct as-is; the
  invariant is that they are never hardcoded anywhere else.
